\begin{abstract}
%==============================================================================
Automated assessment of oil palm Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) ripeness can
standardize harvest decisions, but aggregating plantation imagery centrally
risks exposing commercially sensitive operational data. We present
\textbf{FedX-Palm}, an explainable cross-silo Horizontal Federated Learning
(HFL) framework that couples a YOLOv11n detector for six-class FFB ripeness
detection with Grad-CAM++ explainability and a systematic study of
client-level Differential Privacy (DP). To keep the scope honest, we make
three explicit framing choices. First, the cross-silo design uses $K{=}4$
client nodes with full participation, reflecting a plantation-consortium
deployment scenario rather than a large cross-device federation. Second, the
five-container architecture (one aggregator, four clients, one inference
service) is released as a \emph{Dockerized deployment blueprint}; the
training experiments in this paper are executed as a mathematically
equivalent single-GPU FedAvg simulation, while the trained model is deployed
as a real Docker inference container. Third, privacy budgets are
\emph{computed} from $(\sigma,q,T,\delta)$ via a Rényi DP accountant rather
than labeled by hand. Over five communication rounds with four Non-IID
(Dirichlet) clients and a leakage-free bunch-identity split (9{,}030 training
and 935 validation images / 3{,}140 instances), the non-private federated
model reaches mAP@0.5\,=\,0.995 and mAP@0.5:0.95\,=\,0.951, statistically
matching a centralized benchmark, while Grad-CAM++ yields an Average Drop of
95.1\% and a Focus Retention Rate of 0.962. Adding client-level Gaussian DP
under standard settings (clip-norm $C{=}10$) collapses the global model to
mAP\,=\,0 at every tested noise level. A 12-configuration ablation (three
seeds, zero per-seed variance) isolates the root cause: collapse is governed
by the \emph{absolute per-parameter perturbation magnitude}
$C\!\times\!\sigma$, not by the noise-to-signal ratio, the learning-rate
schedule, or BatchNorm statistics. A simple mitigation---reducing the
clip-bound to $C{=}0.1$---fully restores utility (mAP\,=\,0.995). Because
\emph{all} tested settings sit at $\varepsilon\gg 10^{3}$ (no meaningful
formal privacy at $K{=}4$, $q{=}1$), the paper does \emph{not} claim a
demonstrated privacy--utility trade-off; rather, it characterizes the failure
mode of client-level DP-FedAvg on a pretrained detector in the small-$K$
cross-silo regime and offers a numerically stable mitigation as a starting
point for future privacy-meaningful configurations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%==============================================================================
Oil palm (\emph{Elaeis guineensis Jacq.}) is among the world's most productive
oil crops, and Indonesia and Malaysia consistently lead global palm-oil
production. The quality of the extracted oil depends strongly on harvesting
Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) at the correct maturity. In practice, ripeness is
still judged by visual inspection, which is subjective, inconsistent across
workers, and sensitive to fatigue and field lighting~\cite{septiarini2020classification}.
Deep-learning object detectors such as YOLOv11~\cite{khanam2024yolov11} can
localize and classify each bunch in a single forward pass, making automated,
real-time ripeness assessment feasible.

High-capacity detectors require large training corpora. Aggregating plantation
imagery centrally, however, can leak commercially sensitive
information---geographic coordinates, cultivation patterns, and operational
strategy. Horizontal Federated Learning
(HFL)~\cite{mcmahan2017communication,yang2019federated} addresses this by
keeping raw images on the owning node and exchanging only model updates. Yet
exchanged parameters are not risk-free: membership inference~\cite{shokri2017membership},
model inversion~\cite{fredrikson2015model}, and gradient leakage~\cite{zhu2019deep}
attacks can partially reconstruct sensitive data from weights alone.
Differential Privacy (DP)~\cite{abadi2016deep} provides the strongest formal
remedy, but injecting noise perturbs convergence and typically reduces
accuracy---the \emph{privacy--utility trade-off}. Finally, the
\emph{black-box} nature of deep detectors hampers adoption by agronomists,
motivating Explainable AI (XAI).

This paper integrates these concerns into \textbf{FedX-Palm} and is written
as an \emph{intellectually honest characterization}: each claim is restricted
to what the experiments actually demonstrate. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A federated YOLOv11n detector for six-class FFB ripeness, executed as
a single-GPU FedAvg simulation over four Non-IID clients with a strict
bunch-identity split. The five-service Docker architecture (one server, four
clients, one inference service) is released as a \emph{deployment blueprint},
and the trained model is shipped as a \emph{real} containerized inference
service (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item Quantitative XAI validation of the federated baseline with Grad-CAM++
using the Average Drop and Focus Retention Rate metrics
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item A reproducible characterization of \emph{catastrophic collapse} under
client-level Gaussian DP on a pretrained detector. A 12-configuration
ablation (three seeds) shows the collapse is governed by the absolute
per-parameter perturbation magnitude $C\!\times\!\sigma$, not by the
noise-to-signal ratio, the learning-rate schedule, or BatchNorm statistics;
a small clip-bound ($C{=}0.1$) restores utility numerically, although still
without meaningful formal privacy (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

We explicitly do \emph{not} claim a working privacy-preserving training
configuration: no setting we tested reached a meaningful $\varepsilon$ while
retaining utility. The contribution is a diagnostic foothold for subsequent
work on small-$K$ cross-silo DP.

\section{Related Work}
%==============================================================================
\textbf{Federated learning and Non-IID data.} FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}
established communication-efficient federated optimization; subsequent surveys
\cite{kairouz2021advances,li2020federated} distinguish \emph{cross-device} FL
(thousands to millions of unreliable clients) from \emph{cross-silo} FL (a few
reliable institutional participants). Our plantation-consortium scenario
($K{=}4$ stable nodes) is squarely cross-silo. Statistical heterogeneity is
commonly emulated with a Dirichlet label
partition~\cite{hsu2019measuring}.

\textbf{Differential privacy in FL.} DP-SGD~\cite{abadi2016deep} clips
per-example gradients and adds Gaussian noise, with privacy accounted via
Rényi DP (RDP)~\cite{mironov2017renyi} or zCDP~\cite{bun2016concentrated};
advanced composition~\cite{dwork2010boosting} tightens the linear bound to
order $\sqrt{T}$. At the federation level, DP-FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2018learning}
clips and perturbs the \emph{client update} rather than per-sample gradients.
Wei~\emph{et al.}~\cite{wei2020federated} analyze the resulting
privacy--utility behavior, and LDP-Fed~\cite{truex2020ldp} studies local DP.
A recurring theme is that client-level DP requires a \emph{large} number of
participating clients to retain utility, because privacy amplification by
subsampling scales with the participation ratio $q{=}m/K$; with few clients and
full participation ($q{=}1$) no amplification occurs. Our results corroborate
this regime empirically for object detection.

\textbf{DP for object detection.} Most FL+DP studies target image
classification on MNIST/CIFAR; DP applied to detectors (YOLO/SSD/Faster-RCNN),
whose multi-term losses (localization, classification, distribution focal) are
more perturbation-sensitive, remains sparsely explored. We contribute a
characterized negative result and a practical mitigation in this gap.

\textbf{Explainability.} Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} and
Grad-CAM++~\cite{chattopadhay2018gradcampp} produce gradient-based saliency
maps; faithfulness can be quantified by causal perturbation metrics such as
Average Drop. We adopt Grad-CAM++ on the federated detector and report Average
Drop and Focus Retention Rate.

\textbf{Applied FL.} Federated learning has matured in
healthcare~\cite{rieke2020future} and mobile keyboard
prediction~\cite{hard2018federated}; agricultural deployments remain emerging,
and FedX-Palm contributes a privacy-and-explainability case study for
plantations.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
%==============================================================================
\subsection{System Architecture and Execution Model}
FedX-Palm is designed as a containerized HFL system comprising one FedAvg
\emph{aggregation server} and four \emph{client nodes}, each running in an
isolated Docker container connected over a private bridge network, with
read-only data mounts enforcing a zero-trust boundary~\cite{merkel2014docker}.
To clearly separate design from execution (and to remain reproducible on a
single accelerator), the training experiments in this paper are executed as a
\emph{mathematically equivalent federated simulation}: per-round local training
followed by weighted FedAvg aggregation, identical to the distributed
formulation. The \texttt{docker-compose} architecture serves as a deployment
blueprint, while the trained model is additionally deployed as a \emph{real}
Docker inference container exposing a web endpoint for live detection and
Grad-CAM++ visualization.

\subsection{Dataset and Non-IID Partitioning}
\label{sec:dataset}
The source data is the public Roboflow ``palm-fruit-ripeness-detection''
dataset (version~2) annotated with six classes, in alphabetical order:
\emph{Abnormal}, \emph{Empty Bunch}, \emph{Overripe}, \emph{Ripe},
\emph{Underripe}, and \emph{Unripe}. Because frames originate from short
videos of individual fresh fruit bunches, the vendor's per-frame split allows
near-duplicate frames of the same bunch to appear in train, validation, and
test. We therefore re-split by \emph{bunch identity}
(\texttt{bunch\_id} parsed from filenames) using a stratified group split that
targets an 80/10/10 train/validation/test ratio \emph{at the bunch level} with
a fixed seed; the post-split audit guarantees zero shared bunches and zero
shared filenames across splits. Because bunches contain a variable number of
video frames, the realized \emph{image} counts deviate from the nominal ratio.
All models reported in this paper are scored on a common held-out validation
set of \textbf{935 images (3{,}140 annotated instances)}; a separate held-out
test set is reserved for future deployment evaluation and is not used here.

Statistical heterogeneity is then induced by a Dirichlet label
partition~\cite{hsu2019measuring} of the \emph{training} pool across the four
clients with per-client concentration
$\alpha\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7\}$, yielding pronounced quantity and class skew
(Table~\ref{tab:clients}). The smallest client holds 1{,}145 images and the
largest 3{,}339, for a total of 9{,}030 client training images.

\subsection{Federated Training}
Each client fine-tunes YOLOv11n from pretrained weights for two local epochs
per round using SGD (learning rate $0.01$, batch size $16$, image size
$640{\times}640$), optimizing the standard YOLO loss (CIoU for box regression,
classification loss, and Distribution Focal Loss). The server aggregates client
weights by FedAvg,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}_{t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{N}\,\mathbf{w}_k ,
\end{equation}
where $n_k$ is client $k$'s sample count and $N=\sum_k n_k$. We run $T{=}5$
communication rounds with full participation of all $K{=}4$ clients.

\subsection{Client-Level Differential Privacy}
We implement DP-FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2018learning} at the client level (not
per-sample DP-SGD). Each round, the client update
$\Delta_k=\mathbf{w}_k-\mathbf{w}_t$ is clipped to $\ell_2$-norm $C$ and
perturbed with Gaussian noise before aggregation:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\Delta}_k = \Delta_k \cdot \min\!\Big(1,\tfrac{C}{\lVert \Delta_k \rVert_2}\Big)
\;+\; \mathcal{N}\!\big(0,(\sigma C)^2 \mathbf{I}\big).
\end{equation}
The noise multiplier is $z{=}\sigma$ (since noise std / sensitivity
$=\sigma C / C=\sigma$). We sweep $\sigma\in\{10^{-4},3{\times}10^{-4},
5{\times}10^{-4},10^{-3},3{\times}10^{-3}\}$ at $C{=}10$.

\textbf{Privacy accounting.} Rather than labeling $\varepsilon$ manually, we
compute it from $(\sigma,q,T,\delta)$ via an RDP accountant~\cite{mironov2017renyi},
with full participation $q{=}1$, $T{=}5$ rounds, and $\delta{=}10^{-5}$. For a
non-subsampled Gaussian mechanism, RDP at order $\alpha$ is
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RDP}}(\alpha)=T\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$, converted by
$\varepsilon=\min_{\alpha>1}\big[T\alpha/(2\sigma^2)+\ln(1/\delta)/(\alpha-1)\big]$.
A zCDP cross-check~\cite{bun2016concentrated} agrees within $0.1\%$. As shown in
Section~\ref{sec:results}, all swept $\sigma$ yield $\varepsilon\gg 10^{3}$:
the tested regime carries \emph{no meaningful privacy}, which is central to
interpreting the utility results.

\subsection{Ablation Design}
To diagnose the collapse, we run a 12-configuration ablation, each repeated
with three seeds. The grid isolates candidate causes:
clipping-only ($\sigma{=}0$; $C{\in}\{10,1\}$), noise with varied clip-bound
($C{\in}\{10,1,0.1\}$ at $\sigma{=}5{\times}10^{-3}$), reduced learning rate
($10^{-3},10^{-4}$), a frozen backbone, and replacing BatchNorm with GroupNorm
(with and without noise). The reported quantity is the global mAP@0.5.

\subsection{Explainability}
We apply Grad-CAM++~\cite{chattopadhay2018gradcampp} to the federated baseline
and quantify faithfulness with two metrics: \emph{Average Drop}, the drop in
class confidence when the salient region is \emph{removed} (under this
convention, \emph{higher} indicates a more causal explanation), and the
\emph{Focus Retention Rate} (FRR), the fraction of attention mass on the object
region.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
%==============================================================================
\subsection{Federated Baseline (No DP)}
Initialized from pretrained weights, the global model converges almost
immediately: mAP@0.5 reaches $\approx 0.995$ in round~1 and remains stable
through round~5. The final non-private global model attains
mAP@0.5\,=\,0.995, mAP@0.5:0.95\,=\,0.951, precision\,=\,0.998, and
recall\,=\,0.999 (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). The federated baseline is
statistically indistinguishable from a centralized benchmark at mAP@0.5,
confirming that FedAvg preserves accuracy under Non-IID data without raw images
leaving any node. To check that this near-perfect score reflects genuine
separability rather than an easy majority class, we inspect the centralized
benchmark, which we use as a per-class proxy because the federated logging
records only aggregate global metrics. The per-class AP@0.5 (Table~\ref{tab:perclass})
is uniformly $0.993$--$0.995$, and the normalized confusion matrix
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cm}) is essentially diagonal: \emph{adjacent}-class confusion
between visually similar ripeness stages (e.g., \emph{Ripe} vs.\
\emph{Overripe} vs.\ \emph{Underripe}) is $0.00$. Notably, the minority class
\emph{Empty Bunch}---visibly the smallest cell on the diagonal of
Fig.~\ref{fig:cm}---also attains AP\,=\,0.995, which rules out a majority-class
bias as the explanation for the near-perfect global score. The residual error
is entirely concentrated in the background column---false positives where the
detector fires on background ($0.05$--$0.33$ across classes)---and a single
$0.01$ false negative for \emph{Ripe}. The six ripeness classes are therefore
\emph{visually well-separated} at close range; combined with the
COCO-pretrained initialization, this accounts for both the high accuracy and
the rapid convergence. This flat near-optimum also explains the model's
sensitivity to weight perturbation analyzed below.

\subsection{Effect of Differential Privacy}
Table~\ref{tab:dp} reports the DP sweep with RDP-computed $\varepsilon$, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff} visualizes both per-round convergence and the resulting
privacy--utility curve. \emph{Every} setting drives the global model to
mAP\,=\,0, even at the weakest noise ($\sigma{=}10^{-4}$). Crucially, all
$\varepsilon \gg 10^{3}$: the collapse occurs \emph{before} any
meaningful-privacy regime is reached. The failure is therefore better described
as a configuration/pipeline instability than as a genuine privacy--utility
trade-off. We also observed that local client models remained accurate at small
$\sigma$ while the \emph{aggregate} collapsed, indicating that the perturbation
of the aggregated weights---not local training---is responsible. The curve in
Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}(right) is a sharp \emph{cliff} rather than a gradual
slope: utility drops from 0.995 to 0 with no intermediate operating point.

\subsection{Ablation: Locating the Root Cause}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} summarizes the 12-configuration ablation (mean over
three seeds; per-seed variance was zero). Three findings stand out.

\textbf{(1) Clipping is harmless; noise is the trigger.} Clipping-only at both
$C{=}10$ (A1) and $C{=}1$ (A2) preserves mAP\,=\,0.995. Collapse appears only
when Gaussian noise is added.

\textbf{(2) The absolute perturbation magnitude $C\!\times\!\sigma$, not the
noise/signal ratio, governs collapse.} Configuration A5 ($C{=}0.1$,
$\sigma{=}5{\times}10^{-3}$) preserves mAP\,=\,0.995 even though its
noise-to-signal diagnostic is identical to the collapsing A3/A4. The only
quantity that changes is the per-element noise $C\!\times\!\sigma$:
$5{\times}10^{-2}$ (A3) and $5{\times}10^{-3}$ (A4) collapse, whereas
$5{\times}10^{-4}$ (A5) survives. Combined with the main sweep
($C{=}10,\sigma{=}10^{-4}\!\Rightarrow\!C\!\times\!\sigma{=}10^{-3}$, collapse),
the empirical threshold lies between $5{\times}10^{-4}$ (safe) and
$10^{-3}$ (collapse), i.e.\ the per-element noise must stay below
$\sim\!8\%$ of the per-element signal ($\approx 6.19\times10^{-3}$) so that
five rounds of accumulation do not push the global weights into a
non-functional region.

\textbf{(3) BatchNorm is not the culprit, and non-DP remedies do not help.}
Replacing BatchNorm with GroupNorm and adding noise (C1) still collapses, while
GroupNorm without noise (C1b) trains normally (0.977). Reducing the learning
rate (B1, B2) and freezing the backbone (B3) likewise fail, because the noise
is applied to the \emph{aggregated weights}, not the local gradients. The
practical mitigation is therefore to bound the absolute perturbation directly
via a small clip-bound ($C{=}0.1$).

We stress that $C{=}0.1$ restores \emph{utility}, not \emph{privacy}: it
operates in the same $\varepsilon\gg10^{3}$ regime. Its value is diagnostic
rather than deployable---quantifying the $C\!\times\!\sigma$ collapse threshold
delimits the feasible region within which any $(C,\sigma)$ pair must lie to keep
the detector functional. This is a prerequisite for a privacy-meaningful search:
it tells future work that reaching a useful $\varepsilon$ at fixed utility
cannot be done by tuning $(C,\sigma)$ alone in this regime, and must instead
raise the sensitivity headroom (e.g., far larger $K$ with subsampling
amplification) or lower the required noise at equal privacy (e.g., secure
aggregation), thereby narrowing an otherwise unbounded design space.

\subsection{Why Small-$K$ Client-Level DP Is Hard}
The collapse is consistent with the theory of client-level DP. Privacy
amplification by subsampling scales with $q{=}m/K$; with $K{=}4$ and full
participation $q{=}1$, there is \emph{no} amplification, and the update
sensitivity ($\sim\!C/K$) is large. Achieving a meaningful $\varepsilon$ would
require $\sigma$ orders of magnitude larger than tested---well past the collapse
threshold. This matches long-standing observations that client-level DP needs
many participating clients to retain utility~\cite{mcmahan2018learning,kairouz2021advances}.
Our study confirms this prediction empirically in the under-explored setting of
object detection, and contributes a concrete failure mechanism (absolute
perturbation magnitude) and mitigation (small $C$).

\subsection{Explainability of the Baseline}
On the functional (non-private) baseline, Grad-CAM++ yields an Average Drop of
95.1\% and an FRR of 0.962. Under our convention, the high Average Drop means
that removing the highlighted region collapses the model's confidence by
95.1\%---evidence that the saliency is causal rather than incidental---while
FRR\,=\,0.962 indicates that 96.2\% of the attention mass concentrates on the
fruit region rather than the background. XAI could not be evaluated on the DP
models, which produce no valid predictions.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}
We list the limitations explicitly because each one constrains the strength of
our claims.

\textbf{Execution model.} The federated training is a single-GPU FedAvg
simulation, not a physically distributed run across separate Docker
containers. The aggregation is mathematically identical to distributed
FedAvg, but network effects (latency, stragglers, partial participation,
client drop-out) are absent. The Dockerized architecture is therefore a
deployment \emph{blueprint}, and only the inference service is shown running
as a real container. Validating the framework on a multi-host containerized
deployment with non-trivial network dynamics is left as future work.

\textbf{Optimistic baseline.} The non-private baseline accuracy is unusually
high (mAP@0.5\,=\,0.995). We attribute this to (i)~initializing from a
COCO-pretrained YOLOv11n on a six-class problem whose classes are visually
well-separated bunches at close range, and (ii)~the small communication
horizon ($T{=}5$ rounds) being sufficient because the pretrained init is
already near-optimal. While the bunch-identity split eliminates the dominant
source of leakage, we cannot rule out residual distributional similarity
between splits beyond bunch identity (e.g.\ same plantation, same camera).
\emph{This optimism propagates into the DP analysis}: because the baseline is
on a flat near-optimum, the perturbation sensitivity we measure is an upper
bound for this class of detector, and the absolute mAP gap on collapse is
maximal by construction. The qualitative finding (collapse, governed by
$C\!\times\!\sigma$) is unlikely to change on a harder benchmark, but the
numerical threshold may shift.

\textbf{Limited federated dynamics.} Only $K{=}4$ clients, $T{=}5$ rounds, and
one architecture (YOLOv11n) are studied. The convergence is nearly immediate
in round one, so this setup does not stress-test the federated dynamics
(client drift, partial participation) that motivate much of the FL
literature. We deliberately accept this restriction because it matches the
target cross-silo plantation scenario, but generalization to larger $K$,
longer $T$, or different detector backbones requires further experiments.

\textbf{No demonstrated meaningful privacy.} Every $\sigma$ we tested yields
$\varepsilon \gg 10^{3}$ under the RDP accountant. The $C{=}0.1$ mitigation
restores utility but does \emph{not} reach a privacy-meaningful $\varepsilon$
(we still operate in the same no-privacy regime). The paper therefore
characterizes a failure mode and a numerical stabilizer; it does not
demonstrate a working privacy-preserving configuration. Achieving useful
$\varepsilon$ at $K{=}4$ would likely require secure aggregation, trusted
execution, much larger $K$ via cross-device federation, or a completely
different DP mechanism, all of which are out of scope.

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%==============================================================================
We presented FedX-Palm, an explainable Horizontal Federated Learning framework
for six-class oil palm FFB ripeness detection with YOLOv11n, evaluated as a
single-GPU FedAvg simulation and shipped with a Docker deployment blueprint
plus a real inference container. The non-private federated baseline reaches
mAP@0.5\,=\,0.995 and mAP@0.5:0.95\,=\,0.951 on a leakage-free bunch-identity
split, with Grad-CAM++ confirming morphology-grounded saliency (Average Drop
95.1\%, FRR 0.962). When client-level Gaussian DP is added, the global model
collapses to mAP\,=\,0 across every tested $\sigma$. Our main contribution is
to \emph{characterize, not just report}, this collapse: a 12-configuration
ablation isolates the absolute per-parameter perturbation magnitude
$C\!\times\!\sigma$ as the governing quantity, ruling out the noise/signal
ratio, learning-rate schedule, frozen backbone, and BatchNorm statistics, and
identifies a small clip-bound ($C{=}0.1$) as a numerically stable mitigation
that preserves utility. Because all tested settings lie at
$\varepsilon\gg10^{3}$ under an RDP accountant, the paper does \emph{not}
claim a working privacy-preserving configuration; rather, it provides a
reproducible diagnostic foothold for follow-up work in the difficult
small-$K$ cross-silo regime~\cite{kairouz2021advances}. Future work includes:
(i)~validating the framework on a physically distributed multi-container
deployment with non-trivial network dynamics, (ii)~searching for
$(C,\sigma)$ pairs that reach single-digit $\varepsilon$ while retaining
utility---e.g.\ adaptive clipping with noise warm-up, secure aggregation, or
trusted execution---and (iii)~replicating the diagnosis on harder
benchmarks, larger backbones, and longer communication horizons.
